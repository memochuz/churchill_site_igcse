## What are Bearings?

A **bearing** is a way of describing direction using angles.

- Bearings are always measured:
  - From **North**
  - In a **clockwise direction**

## How are Bearings Written?

Bearings are written as **three-digit angles**:

- $030^\circ$ (NOT $30^\circ$)
- $090^\circ$
- $270^\circ$

Always include **three digits**

---

## Drawing Bearings

To draw a bearing **from** $A$ to $B$:

1. Connect points $A$ and $B$.
2. Draw a **North line** at point $A$. The word **from** indicates the starting point, so this is where the North line must be drawn.
3. Place your **protractor at the starting point** and measure the angle **clockwise**.

#### Example: Finding a Distance

A ship travels:

- $10$ km on a bearing of $090^\circ$  
- Then $10$ km on a bearing of $105^\circ$

<p align="center">
   <img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/Geometry/bearing_problem.png" width="600">
</p>

Determine the distance between the ship’s final position and its point of origin. This can be done by forming a triangle and applying trigonometric methods.

First, we determine the angle $B$. Since the bearing from $A$ is $090^\circ$, we can calculate:

$$
B = 360^\circ - 90^\circ - 105^\circ = 165^\circ.
$$

Next, we use the cosine rule to find the distance between $A$ and $C$:

$$
AC = \sqrt{10^2 + 10^2 - 2(10)(10)\cos(165^\circ)} \approx 19.83.
$$

## Common Mistakes

- Measuring from East instead of North  
- Going anticlockwise  
- Writing $30^\circ$ instead of $030^\circ$  

---

## Summary

- Bearings measure direction  
- Measured from **North clockwise**  
- Always written with **three digits**
- Can form triangles  
- Can be solved using trigonometry  
- Used in real-life situations (navigation, maps, GPS)
