## Parts of a Circle

#### Important Lines

<p align="center">
   <img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/Geometry/lines.png" width="600">
</p>

Understanding the parts of a circle helps us understand many of the geometric properties used in circle theorems.

* **Centre**: The point that is the same distance from every point on the circle.
* **Radius**: The distance from the centre to any point on the circumference.
* **Diameter**: A chord that passes through the centre of the circle.  
  It is equal to **twice the radius**.
* **Circumference**: The distance around the edge of the circle.
* **Chord**: A straight line segment joining two points on the circumference.
* **Tangent**: A straight line that touches the circle at exactly **one point**.

## Circle Theorems

Circle theorems describe **special relationships between angles, chords, tangents, and radii**. They are commonly used to find **unknown angles in geometric problems**.

#### Core Circle Theorems

These are the **most commonly used circle theorems in exams**.



###### Angles in a Semicircle

The angle subtended by a **diameter** at the circumference is always a **right angle**.

<p align="center">
   <img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/Geometry/diameter_right.png" width="600">
</p>

In other words, if a triangle is formed using the diameter as one side, the angle opposite the diameter is always:

$$
90^\circ
$$



###### Radius and Tangent

A **radius** drawn to the point where a tangent touches the circle is **perpendicular** to the tangent.

<p align="center">
   <img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/Geometry/radius_tangent.png" width="600">
</p>

$$
\text{Radius} \perp \text{Tangent}
$$



###### Two Tangents from the Same Point

If two tangents are drawn from the **same external point**, their lengths are **equal**.

<p align="center">
   <img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/Geometry/tangents.png" width="600">
</p>

This creates two **congruent triangles**, which is often useful when solving angle problems.



###### Isosceles Triangles in Circles

If two radii are joined to the endpoints of a chord, an **isosceles triangle** is formed.

<p align="center">
   <img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/Geometry/isosceles.png" width="600">
</p>

Since all radii are equal:

$$
\text{Base angles are equal}
$$



###### Radius Bisecting a Chord

If a radius **bisects a chord**, then it meets the chord at **\(90^\circ\)**.

<p align="center">
   <img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/Geometry/bisect.png" width="600">
</p>

This theorem is useful for identifying **right triangles inside circles**.



#### Extended Circle Theorems

These theorems appear frequently in **more advanced circle problems**.


###### Equal Chords

If two chords are **equal in length**, they are the **same distance from the centre** of the circle.

<p align="center">
   <img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/Geometry/chords.png" width="600">
</p>



###### Angle at the Centre

The angle subtended by an arc at the **centre of the circle** is **twice the angle** subtended at the **circumference**.

<p align="center">
   <img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/Geometry/central_angle.png" width="600">
</p>

$$
\text{Central angle} = 2 \times \text{Angle at the circumference}
$$


###### Angles in the Same Segment

Angles at the circumference that **subtend the same chord** are **equal**.

<p align="center">
   <img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/Geometry/angle_circunference.png" width="600">
</p>

The word **subtend** means that the angle is formed by lines connecting its vertex to the endpoints of the same chord or arc.



###### Cyclic Quadrilateral

A **cyclic quadrilateral** is a four-sided polygon whose vertices lie on the circumference of a circle.

The opposite angles are **supplementary**:

$$
A + C = 180^\circ
$$

<p align="center">
   <img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/Geometry/ciclico.png" width="600">
</p>



###### Alternate Segment Theorem

The angle between a **tangent and a chord** is equal to the angle in the **opposite arc**.

<p align="center">
   <img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/Geometry/alternate.png" width="600">
</p>

This theorem connects **tangent geometry with angles inside the circle**.

## Common Exam Strategy

A useful strategy for solving circle theorem problems:

1. Identify all **radii**.
2. Look for **isosceles triangles**.
3. Check for **diameters**.
4. Look for **angles subtending the same chord**.
5. Check if a **cyclic quadrilateral** appears.

Most circle theorem problems require **combining several theorems together**.
