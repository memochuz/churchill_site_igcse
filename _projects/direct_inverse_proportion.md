## Proportional Relationships

Two quantities are **proportional** when one depends on the other in a consistent way.

---

## Direct Proportion

Two quantities are in **direct proportion** if:

> As **one increases**, the other **also increases** (or both decrease).

If this is the case, we write:

$$
y \propto x
$$

This symbol $\propto$ is often called the “proportional to” symbol.



#### Equation Form

If $y$ is directly proportional to $x$, $y \propto x$, we can express this relationship as:

$$
y = kx
$$

Where:
* $k$ is the constant of proportionality  

Once we know the value of $k$, we can use this equation to find $y$ for any given value of $x$, or vice versa.

This equation preserves the direct relationship between $y$ and $x$: as $x$ increases, $y$ increases proportionally, and as $x$ decreases, $y$ decreases proportionally.

The constant $k$ determines how quickly $y$ changes with respect to $x$; in other words, it represents the **rate of change** (or slope) of the relationship. For example, if $k$ is large, a small change in $x$ will result in a **large change** in $y$, and if $k$ is small, a small change in $x$ will result in a **small change** in $y$.



#### Example

If $y \propto x$, and $y = 10$ when $x = 2$, find $k$:

$$
y = kx \Rightarrow 10 = k(2) \Rightarrow k = \frac{10}{2} = 5
$$

So:

$$
y = 5x
$$

---

## Inverse Proportion

Two quantities are in **inverse proportion** if:

> As **one increases**, the other **decreases**.

We write:   

$$
y \propto \frac{1}{x}
$$



#### Equation Form

If $y$ is inversely proportional to $x$, $y \propto \frac{1}{x}$, we can express this relationship as:

$$
y = \frac{k}{x}
$$

Where:
* $k$ is the constant of proportionality

Once again, knowing $k$ allows us to find $y$ for any given value of $x$, or vice versa. And from the equation is obvious that as $x$ increases, $y$ decreases, and as $x$ decreases, $y$ increases, which is the hallmark of an inverse relationship.



#### Example

If $y \propto \frac{1}{x}$ and $y = 6$ when $x = 2$, find $k$:

$$
y = \frac{k}{x} \Rightarrow 6 = \frac{k}{2} \Rightarrow k = 6 \times 2  = 12
$$

So:

$$
y = \frac{12}{x}
$$

## How to Solve Problems

#### Direct and Inverse Proportion

1. Write:  $y = kx$  or  $y = \frac{k}{x}$ 
2. Substitute known values  
3. Solve for $k$  
4. Find the unknown  


### Example 1 (Direct)

A car travels $120$ km in $2$ hours. How far will it travel in $5$ hours?

Since distance is directly proportional to time,  $distance \propto time$, we use the direct proportion formula:

$$
y = kx
$$

where,
* $y$ is the distance traveled
* $x$ is the time taken

Therefore, we can find $k$ using the given values:

$$
k = \frac{120}{2} = 60
$$

Finally, we can find the distance traveled in $5$ hours:

$$
y = 60 \cdot 5 = 300
$$

---

### Example 2 (Inverse)

4 workers complete a task in 6 days. How long will 8 workers take?

Workers and time are inversely proportional since more workers means less time (and vice versa). So we use the inverse proportion formula:

$$
y = \frac{k}{x}
$$

where,
* $y$ is the time taken
* $x$ is the number of workers

Therefore, we can find $k$ using the given values:

$$
k = 4 \cdot 6 = 24
$$

Finally, we can find the time taken for 8 workers:

$$
y = \frac{24}{8} = 3
$$

## Common Mistakes

* Confusing direct and inverse proportion  
* Forgetting to find $k$ first  
* Using the wrong formula  
* Not checking if the relationship makes sense
