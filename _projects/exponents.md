# **Laws of Exponents**

In this lesson, we will learn the **laws or properties of exponents**, which allow us to simplify and manipulate expressions with powers.

---

## **1. Basic Definitions**

If $a$ is a nonzero real number and $m$, $n$ are integers, powers are defined as:

- **Positive exponent**: $a^n = \underbrace{a \cdot a \cdot a \ldots \cdot a}_{\text{$n$ times}}$ 
- **Zero exponent**: $a^0 = 1$
- **Negative exponent**: $a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}$

⚠️ **Note**: The last property **do not apply** if the base $a$ is zero and the exponent is negative or zero, because division by zero is undefined.

---

## **2. Laws and Step-by-Step Examples**

### **a) Zero Exponent**
$$
a^0 = 1
$$

⚠️ **Note**: As long as $ a \neq 0 $.

**Example**:  
$ 6^0 = 1 $

---

### **b) Exponent One**
$$
a^1 = a
$$

This means that any number raised to the power of one is itself.

**Example**:  
$ 7^1 = 7 $

---

### **c) Product of Powers (Same Base)**
$$
a^{k} \times a^{m} = a^{k+m}
$$


**Example**:  
$$
5^{12} \times 5^{-4} = 5^{12 + (-4)} = 5^{8}
$$

---

### **d) Quotient of Powers (Same Base)**
$$
\frac{a^{k}}{a^{m}} = a^{k-m}
$$

**Example**:  
$$
\frac{5^{12}}{5^{4}} = 5^{12 - 4} = 5^{8}
$$


---

### **e) Power of a Power**
$$
(a^{k})^{m} = a^{k \cdot m}
$$

**Example**:  
$$
(3^{3})^{-5} = 3^{3 \cdot (-5)} = 3^{-15}
$$

---

### **f) Negative Exponent**
$$
a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}
$$


**Example**:  
$$
5^{-4} = \frac{1}{5^4} = \frac{1}{625}
$$

---

### **g) Reciprocal of a Negative Power**
$$
\frac{1}{a^{-n}} = a^n
$$

**Example**:  
$$
\frac{1}{5^{-4}} = 5^{4} = 625
$$

---

### **h) Power of a Product**
$$
(a \cdot b)^n = a^n \cdot b^n
$$

**Example**:  
$$
(10 \cdot 3)^4 = 10^4 \cdot 3^4 = 10000 \cdot 81 = 810000
$$

---

### **i) Power of a Quotient**
$$
\left( \frac{a}{b} \right)^n = \frac{a^n}{b^n}
$$

**Example**:  
$$
\left( \frac{5}{6} \right)^{-2} = \frac{5^{-2}}{6^{-2}} = \frac{\frac{1}{25}}{\frac{1}{36}} = \frac{36}{25}
$$

---

### **j) Combining Laws**
$$
\frac{a^{k} \cdot c^{-n}}{b} = \frac{a^{k}}{b \cdot c^{n}}
$$


**Example**:  
$$
\frac{2 \cdot 3^{-4}}{5} = \frac{2}{5 \cdot 3^4} = \frac{2}{405}
$$

---

## **3. Tips and Common Mistakes**
- **Same base**: The addition or subtraction of exponents only applies if the base is identical.
- **Order of operations**: Powers are calculated before multiplying or dividing, unless parentheses indicate otherwise.
- **Negative exponent ≠ negative number**: $2^{-3}$ is not $-8$, it is $\frac{1}{8}$.
