## What is Factorization?

Factorization means writing an expression as a **product of factors**.

**Example:**

$$
6x + 9 = 3(2x + 3)
$$

---

## Common Factor

We take out the **greatest common factor (GCF)** from all terms.

**Steps**:
1. Find the common number and/or variable  
2. Factor it out  


###### Example 1
Factorize:

$$
8x + 12
$$

Common factor $= 4$

$$
8x + 12 = 4(2x + 3)
$$


###### Example 2

Factorize:

$$
6x^2 + 9x
$$

Common factor $= 3x$

$$
6x^2 + 9x = 3x(2x + 3)
$$

## Quadratic Factorization

##### Case 1: Leading Coefficient = 1

Form:
$$
x^2 + bx + c
$$

Find two numbers that:
* Multiply to give **c**  
* Add to give **b**
* Then write two brackets using $x$ and the numbers you found


###### Example

Factorize:

$$
x^2 + 5x + 6
$$

Numbers: $2$ and $3$, since $2\times 3 = 6$ and $2+3=5$

$$
x^2 + 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 3)
$$


#### Case 2: Leading Coefficient ≠ 1

Form:
$$
ax^2 + bx + c
$$

**Steps**:
1. Multiply **a x c**
2. Find two numbers that multiply to this and add to **b**
3. Split the middle term  with the numbers previously found
4. Factor by grouping (you'll have to do this two times)


#### Example

Factorize:

$$
2x^2 + 7x + 3
$$

**Step 1:**

$$
a \times c = 2 \times 3 = 6
$$

**Step 2:**  

Numbers: $\textcolor{#FF5555}{6}$ and $\textcolor{#FFB86C}{1}$, since $6\times 1 = 6$ and $6+1 =7$

**Step 3:**
Split the middle term:

$$
2x^2 + \underbrace{\textcolor{#FF5555}{6}x + \textcolor{#FFB86C}{1}x}_{7x} + 3
$$

**Step 4:**
Factorize completely until the expression is written as a product of two linear polynomials.
$$
2x\underbrace{\textcolor{#BD93F9}{(x + 3)}}_{\text{factorize}} + 1\underbrace{\textcolor{#BD93F9}{(x + 3)}}_{\text{factorize}}
$$

**Final:**

$$
(2x + 1)(x + 3)
$$

##  Difference of Squares

We can factorize using the difference of squares formula, which is shown below:

#### Formula:
$$
a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b),
$$

when we have a subtraction of two terms that can each be written as a square, we can apply this formula. The goal is to remove the squares and rewrite the expression as a product of linear factors.

### Example 1

Factorize:

$$
x^2 - 9
$$

Using the formula we obtain:

$$
x^2 - 9 = \underbrace{(x - 3)}_{\text{linear}}\underbrace{(x + 3)}_{\text{linear}}
$$


### Example 2

Example:

$$
3x^2 - 25y^{2}
$$

Using the formula we obtain:

$$
3x^2 - 25y^{2} = \underbrace{(\sqrt{3}x - 5y)}_{\text{linear}} \underbrace{(\sqrt{3}x + 5y)}_{\text{linear}}
$$

## Common Mistakes

* Not taking out the greatest common factor  
* Wrong pair of numbers in quadratics  
* Forgetting to check both multiply AND add  
* Not recognising difference of squares
