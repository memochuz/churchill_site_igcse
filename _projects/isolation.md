# Lesson: Rearranging Equations (Isolating a Variable)

---

## 1. What does it mean to rearrange (isolate) a variable?

To **rearrange an equation** means to manipulate it in order to **isolate one variable** on one side of the equation (usually the left side).  
This allows us to clearly express the chosen variable in terms of the others.  

For example, isolate $x$:  

$$2x + 5y = 11 \quad \Rightarrow \quad \underbrace{x = \frac{11-5y}{2}}_{\text{$x$ is isolated}}$$  

---

## 2. Using inverse operations to isolate a variable

To isolate a variable, we use **inverse operations** (operations that "undo" each other).  

- Addition ↔ Subtraction  
- Multiplication ↔ Division  
- Exponentiation ↔ Root (radical)  

---

## 3. Step by step process for rearranging

When isolating a variable:  

1. **Identify the operations** directly affecting the variable.  

2. **Undo the operations in reverse order**, moving terms to the other side with the inverse operation.  

3. **Simplify** if possible.  
  
For example, isolate $y$ from the following equation:

    $$ x = \sqrt[3]{\frac{3x^{3}}{4}} $$

### Step 1: Identify the operations affecting $y$ most immediatly

1. The exponent \(3\):  $\sqrt[3]{\frac{3x^{\textcolor{#FF5555
}{3}}}{4}}$
2. Multiplication by \(3\):  $\sqrt[3]{\frac{\textcolor{#FF5555}{3}x^{3}}{4}}$
3. Division by \(4\):  $\sqrt[3]{\frac{3x^{3}}{\textcolor{#FF5555}{4}}}$
4. The cubic root: $\sqrt[\textcolor{#FF5555}{3}]{\frac{3x^{3}}{4}}$

---

### Step 2: Undo the operations in reverse order

1. **Remove the square root** by applying the opposite operation (square) on the other side:  

   $$ x^{\textcolor{#FF5555}{3}} = \frac{3y^{3}}{4} $$  

2. **Remove the division by 4** by multiplying on the other side:  

   $$ \textcolor{#FF5555}{4}x^{3} = 3y^{3} $$  

3. **Remove the multiplication by 3** by dividing on the other side:  

   $$ \frac{4x^3}{\textcolor{#FF5555}{3}} = y^{3} $$  

4. **Remove the exponent 3** by applying the cube root on the other side: 

   $$ y = \sqrt[\textcolor{#FF5555}{3}]{\frac{4x^3}{3}} $$  


---

## More Examples

1.  
$$ \frac{a}{5} + 2 = 7 $$  

Step 1: Subtract 2 → $ \frac{a}{5} = 7-2 $

Step 2: Multiply by 5 → $ a = (7-2)5 $

Step 3: Simplify → $ a = 25$ 

2.  

$$ \sqrt{m} - 3 = 1 $$  

Step 1: Add 3 → $ \sqrt{m} = (1+3) $ 

Step 2: Square → $ m = (1+3) ^{2} $ 

Step 3: Simplify  → $ m = 16$
