## What is a Percentage?

A **percentage** means “out of 100”.

$$
25\% = \frac{25}{100} = 0.25
$$

---

## Finding a Percentage of a Quantity

To find a percentage of a number:

$$
\text{Value} = \frac{\text{percentage}}{100} \times \text{total}
$$


#### Example

Find $20\%$ of $150$:

$$
\frac{20}{100} \times 150 = 30
$$

---

## Percentage Increase and Decrease

#### Increase

$$
\text{New Value} = \text{Original} \times \left(1 + \frac{\text{percentage}}{100}\right)
$$


#### Decrease

$$
\text{New Value} = \text{Original} \times \left(1 - \frac{\text{percentage}}{100}\right)
$$



#### Example

Increase $200$ by $10\%$:

$$
200 \times 1.10 = 220
$$

Decrease $200$ by $10\%$:

$$
200 \times 0.90 = 180
$$

## Rule of Three

The rule of three is a helpful method for solving proportional problems. However, it is important to know when and how to apply it correctly. Essentially, it is based on an equality between two ratios and allows us to find a missing value when three values are already known.

There are two common cases: the unknown value can appear either in the numerator or in the denominator.


$$
\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{x} \quad \text{or} \quad \frac{a}{b} = \frac{x}{d}
$$

In both cases, we need to isolate the variable $x$. This is done by cross-multiplying (multiplying diagonally using the known values) and then dividing by the remaining value.

That is:

$$
x = \frac{b \cdot c}{a} \quad \text{or} \quad x = \frac{a \cdot d}{b}
$$

---

## Reverse Percentages

Used when the final value is known and we need to find the original value before a percentage increase or decrease was applied.

#### Example

After a $20\%$ increase, the price is $120$. Find the original.

1. This means that $120$ represents $120\%$ of the original price, and we need to find the original value $x$, which corresponds to $100\%$.

2. First, we write the equation:

    $$
    \frac{1.20}{120} = \frac{1}{x}
    $$

3. Then, we solve for $x$:

    $$
    \text{x} = \frac{120 \times 1}{1.20} = 100
    $$

## Common Mistakes

* Confusing increase with decrease  
* Using wrong values in rule of three
