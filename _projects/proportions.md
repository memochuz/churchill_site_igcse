## What is a Ratio?

A **ratio** compares two or more quantities.

It shows how much of one thing there is **compared to another**.

**Example:**

$$3 : 2$$


This means:
* For every 3 parts of A  
* There are 2 parts of B  

---

## Simplifying Ratios

Ratios should be written in their **simplest form**.

### Steps:
1. Write the ratio  
2. Divide all parts by the **highest common factor (HCF)**  

**Example:**

* $12 : 8 \rightarrow \text{divide by } 4 \rightarrow 3 : 2$


---

## Ratios with Units

Make sure all quantities are in the **same units** before forming a ratio.

**Example:**

* $2 \, m : 50 \, cm \rightarrow 200 \, cm : 50 \, cm \rightarrow 4 : 1$


---

## Dividing in a Given Ratio

We often need to divide a total amount according to a ratio.

#### Steps:
1. Add the parts of the ratio  
2. Divide the total by this sum  
3. Multiply by each part  

**Example:**

Divide $60$ in the ratio $2 : 3$

* Total parts $= 2 + 3 = 5$
* Each part $= 60 ÷ 5 = 12 $ 

* First share $= 2 × 12 = 24 $ 
* Second share $= 3 × 12 = 36 $ 

*Explanation*:

This means that $60$ is divided into $5$ equal parts, each worth $12$.

Then:
* One quantity gets $2$ parts → $2\times 12=24$
* The other gets $3$ parts → $3\times 12=36$

---

## What is Proportion?

Two ratios are in **proportion** if they are equal.


$$ 2 : 3 = 4 : 6 $$


This can also be written as:

$$
\frac{2}{3} = \frac{4}{6}
$$

---

## Typical IGCSE Problems

#### Sharing in a Ratio

Ali and Sara share $84$ in the ratio $5 : 2$.  
Find how much each person gets.

*Solution*:

* Total parts $= 5 + 2 = 7$  
* Value of each part $= 84 \div 7 = 12$  

* Ali’s share $= 5 \times 12 = 60$  
* Sara’s share $= 2 \times 12 = 24$

 
Ali gets $60$ and Sara gets $24$.

#### Missing Value in Proportion

Consider the proportion

$$
\frac{x}{8}=\frac{6}{12}
$$

Find x.

*Solution*:

We just need to isolate $x$

$$
x = \frac{6\times 8}{12} = 4
$$


#### Mixing Ratios

A drink is made using juice and water in the ratio 1 : 4.  
How much juice is needed to make $500 \, \text{ml}$ of the drink?

*Solution*:

* Total parts $= 1 + 4 = 5$ 
* Value of each part $= 500 \div 5 = 100$  

* Juice $= 1 \times 100 = 100 \, \text{ml}$  

 
$100 \, \text{ml}$ of juice is needed.

---

## Common Mistakes

* Not simplifying ratios  
* Mixing units (cm, m, kg, etc.)  
* Adding ratio parts incorrectly  
* Forgetting to divide total first when sharing
