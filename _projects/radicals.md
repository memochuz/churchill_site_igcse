# **Laws of Radicals**

In this lesson, we will learn the **laws or properties of radicals**, which allow us to simplify and manipulate expressions with roots.

---

## **1. Basic Definitions**

If $a$ is a nonnegative real number and $n$ is a positive integer, the **n-th root** of $a$ is defined as:

- **n-th root**:  
$$
\sqrt[n]{a} = b \quad \text{if and only if} \quad b^n = a
$$
- **Square root** ($n=2$): $\sqrt{a}$  
- **Cube root** ($n=3$): $\sqrt[3]{a}$  

⚠️ **Note**:  
- For even $n$, $\sqrt[n]{a}$ is only defined for $a \geq 0$ in real numbers.  
- Roots can be rewritten as fractional exponents:  
$$
\sqrt[n]{a} = a^{\frac{1}{n}}
$$

---

## **2. Laws and Step-by-Step Examples**

### **a) Product Rule**
$$
\sqrt[n]{a \cdot b} = \sqrt[n]{a} \cdot \sqrt[n]{b}
$$

**Example**:  
$$
\sqrt{4 \cdot 9} = \sqrt{4} \cdot \sqrt{9} = 2 \cdot 3 = 6
$$

---

### **b) Quotient Rule**
$$
\sqrt[n]{\frac{a}{b}} = \frac{\sqrt[n]{a}}{\sqrt[n]{b}}
$$

**Example**:  
$$
\sqrt[3]{\frac{8}{27}} = \frac{\sqrt[3]{8}}{\sqrt[3]{27}} = \frac{2}{3}
$$

---

### **c) Power Rule**
$$
\sqrt[n]{a^m} = a^{\frac{m}{n}}
$$

**Example**:  
$$
\sqrt[4]{81^3} = 81^{\frac{3}{4}} = (3^4)^{\frac{3}{4}} = 3^3 = 27
$$

---

### **d) Nested Radicals**
$$
\sqrt[m]{\sqrt[n]{a}} = \sqrt[mn]{a}
$$

**Example**:  
$$
\sqrt[3]{\sqrt[2]{5}} = \sqrt[6]{5}
$$

---

### **e) Simplifying Radicals**
Factor perfect powers out of the radical.

**Example**:  
$$
\sqrt{72} = \sqrt{36 \cdot 2} = \sqrt{36} \cdot \sqrt{2} = 6\sqrt{2}
$$

---

## **3. Tips and Common Mistakes**
- **No separate square root of a sum/difference**:  
$\sqrt{a + b} \neq \sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b}$ in general.  
- **Only factor perfect powers**: Make sure the factor you take out of the radical is indeed a perfect $n$-th power.  

---

\frac{\sqrt[3]{a^{6}} \cdot \sqrt[3]{a^{3}b^{9}}{\sqrt{a^{8}b^{16}}}
$$
and express the result in simplest radical form.
