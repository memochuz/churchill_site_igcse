# CASE 1: Rationalizing Fractions with Simple Radicals  

When a fraction has a radical in the denominator, the goal is to remove the radical and write the fraction with a rational denominator.  

---

## Steps to Rationalize  

1. **Identify the radical in the denominator**  
   Look at the denominator and check if it contains a square root.  

2. **Multiply numerator and denominator by the radical**  
   To remove the radical, multiply top and bottom by the same square root.  
   This is like multiplying by 1, so the value of the fraction does not change.  

3. **Apply the rule**  
   Remember:  
   $$
   \sqrt{a}\cdot \sqrt{a} = (\sqrt{a})^2 = a 
   $$  
   This ensures the denominator becomes rational.  

4. **Simplify if possible**  
   Reduce the fraction or simplify any radicals left in the numerator.  

---

## Example 1  

$$
\frac{5}{\sqrt{3}}
$$

**Step 1:** Identify the radical → denominator is $\sqrt{3}$.  

**Step 2:** Multiply numerator and denominator by $\sqrt{3}$:  

$$
\frac{5}{\sqrt{3}} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3}}
$$

**Step 3:** Apply the rule $\sqrt{3}\cdot \sqrt{3} = 3$:  

$$
\frac{5\sqrt{3}}{3}
$$

**Step 4:** No further simplification needed.  

**Final Answer:**  
$$
\frac{5\sqrt{3}}{3}
$$  

---

## Example 2  

$$
\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}
$$

**Step 1:** Radical = $\sqrt{5}$.  

**Step 2:** Multiply top and bottom by $\sqrt{5}$:  

$$
\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{5}}{\sqrt{5}}
$$

**Step 3:** Simplify denominator:  

$$
\frac{2\sqrt{5}}{5}
$$

**Final Answer:**  
$$
\frac{2\sqrt{5}}{5}
$$

# CASE 2: Rationalizing Fractions with Conjugates  

When a fraction has a denominator with two terms involving radicals (for example $\sqrt{a} + b$), we cannot rationalize by multiplying with just the radical. Instead, we use the **conjugate**.  

The **conjugate** of a binomial is formed by changing the sign between its two terms.  

- Conjugate of $(a + b)$ is $(a - b)$  
- Conjugate of $(a - b)$ is $(a + b)$  

Multiplying by the conjugate uses the **difference of squares formula**:  

$$
(a+b)(a-b) = a^2 - b^2
$$  

This eliminates the radicals from the denominator.  

---

## Steps to Rationalize  

1. **Identify the denominator with two terms (binomial)**  
   Example: $\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{2}$.  

2. **Multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate**  
   This uses the difference of squares to remove radicals in the denominator. 

3. **Apply the difference of squares formula**  
   Use the formula $(a+b)(a-b) = a^2 - b^2$ to simplify the expression.

4. **Simplify if possible**  
   Expand numerator, simplify radicals, and reduce the fraction if needed.  

---

## Example 1  

$$
\frac{1}{\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{2}}
$$  

Let's follow the steps.

**Step 1:** Denominator = $\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{2}$ and conjugate = $\sqrt{3} - \sqrt{2}$.  

**Step 3:** Multiply top and bottom by conjugate:  

$$
\frac{1}{\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{2}} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3} - \sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{3} - \sqrt{2}}
$$  

**Step 4:** Apply difference of squares:  

Denominator: $(\sqrt{3})^2 - (\sqrt{2})^2 = 3 - 2 = 1$  

Numerator: $1 \cdot (\sqrt{3} - \sqrt{2}) = \sqrt{3} - \sqrt{2}$  

**Final Answer:**  
$$
\frac{\sqrt{3} - \sqrt{2}}{1}=\sqrt{3} - \sqrt{2}
$$  

---

## Example 2  

$$
\frac{5}{\sqrt{7} - \sqrt{5}}
$$  

**Step 1:** Denominator = $\sqrt{7} - \sqrt{5}$.  

**Step 2:** Conjugate = $\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{5}$.  

**Step 3:** Multiply top and bottom:  

$$
\frac{5}{\sqrt{7} - \sqrt{5}} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{5}}{\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{5}}
$$  

**Step 4:** Simplify denominator:  

$(\sqrt{7})^2 - (\sqrt{5})^2 = 7 - 5 = 2$  

Numerator: $5(\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{5}) = 5\sqrt{7} + 5\sqrt{5}$  

**Final Answer:**  
$$
\frac{5\sqrt{7} + 5\sqrt{5}}{2}
$$
