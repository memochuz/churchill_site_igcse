## What is Scientific Notation?

Scientific notation is a way of writing numbers in the form:


$$
a × 10^{n}
$$

Where:
* $1 \leq a < 10$ 
* $n$ is an integer  

---

## Converting to Scientific Notation

**Steps**:

1. Move the decimal point so that the first number is between 1 and 10  
2. Count how many places you moved the decimal  point
3. Write this as a power of 10  



#### Example (Large Number)

$$
4500 = 4.5 × 10^{3}
$$

* Decimal moved **3 places to the left** → exponent is **+3**

---

#### Example (Small Number)

$$
0.0062 = 6.2 × 10^{-3}
$$


* Decimal moved **3 places to the right** → exponent is **−3**

---

## Converting Back to Decimal Notation

#### Steps:
* If the exponent is **positive** → move decimal to the **right**  
* If the exponent is **negative** → move decimal to the **left**  


#### Examples


* $3.2 × 10^{4} = 32000$
* $7.1 × 10^{-3} = 0.0071$

---

## Calculations with Scientific Notation

#### Multiplication

Multiply the numbers and add the exponents.

$$
(2 × 10³)(3 × 10²) = 6 × 10^{5}
$$


#### Division

Divide the numbers and subtract the exponents.

$$
(6 × 10^{5}) ÷ (2 × 10^{2}) = 3 × 10^{3}
$$


#### Addition and Subtraction

If the exponents are different:

**Steps**:

1. Rewrite one number so both have the **same exponent**  
2. Add or subtract the coefficients  
3. Keep the common power of 10  
4. Convert back to proper scientific notation if needed  

$$
(3 \times 10^{4}) + (5 \times 10^{3})
$$

Make exponents the same:

$$
5 \times 10^{3} = 0.5 \times 10^{4}
$$

Now add:

$$
(3 \times 10^{4}) + (0.5 \times 10^{4}) = (3 + 0.5) \times 10^{4} = 3.5 \times 10^{4} 
$$


---


## Common Mistakes

* Not making the first number between 1 and 10  
* Forgetting negative exponents for small numbers  
* Moving the decimal in the wrong direction  
* Incorrect exponent when multiplying or dividing
