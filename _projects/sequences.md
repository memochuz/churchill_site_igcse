## 1. Arithmetic Sequences

An **arithmetic sequence** is a sequence of numbers where each term is generated by **adding a constant** $d$ to the previous term. This constant $d$ is called the **common difference** ($d$ could be positive or negative).

### Example of Construction:

$$
\begin{aligned}
U_1 &= \text{initial term} \\
U_2 &= U_1 + d \\
U_3 &= U_2 + d = U_1 + 2d \\
U_4 &= U_3 + d = U_1 + 3d \\
&\vdots \\
U_n &= U_{n-1} + d = U_1 + (n - 1)d
\end{aligned}
$$

Below is an image that shows the construction of an arithmetic sequence using the common difference, $d$, as the "jump." To get to $U_2$, we jump a distance of $d$ from $U_1$. To generate $U_3$, we jump two times, which is a distance of $2d$ from $U_1$. This pattern continues for subsequent terms.

To verify that a sequence is arithmetic, check that the difference between consecutive terms is constant:

$$
d = U_2 - U_1 = U_3 - U_2 = U_4 - U_3 = \ldots
$$

### General Formulas:

- Recursive relation:  
  $$U_n = U_{n-1} + d$$
- Explicit formula or **n-th term**:  
  $$U_n = U_1 + (n - 1)d$$

## 2. Arithmetic Series

An **arithmetic series** is the **sum of the terms** of an arithmetic sequence. For example:

$$S_n = U_1 + U_2 + \ldots + U_n$$

Some example sums:

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_1 &= U_1 \\
S_2 &= U_1 + U_2 \\
S_3 &= U_1 + U_2 + U_3 \\
&\vdots \\
S_n &= U_1 + U_2 + U_3 + \ldots + U_n =\sum_{k=1}^{n} U_k
\end{aligned}
$$

### Sum Formulas:


1. $S_n = \frac{n}{2}(2U_1 + (n - 1)d)$
2. $S_n = \frac{n}{2}(U_1 + U_n)$

We will use one formula or the other depending on the information given in the problem.

### Remark

To solve some of the problems regarding arithmetic sequences and series you might need to find the solution of a system of equations.

## 3. Geometric Sequences

A **geometric sequence** is one where each term is obtained by **multiplying a constant** $r$ to the previous term. This constant $r$ is called the **common ratio** ($r$ could be positive or negative).

### Example of Construction:

$$
\begin{aligned}
U_1 &= \text{initial term} \\
U_2 &= U_1 \cdot r \\
U_3 &= U_2 \cdot r = U_1 \cdot r^2 \\
U_4 &= U_3 \cdot r = U_1 \cdot r^3 \\
&\vdots \\
U_n &= U_{n-1} \cdot r = U_1 \cdot r^{n - 1}
\end{aligned}
$$

To verify a sequence is geometric, the ratio between consecutive terms must be constant:

$$
r = \frac{U_2}{U_1} = \frac{U_3}{U_2} = \frac{U_4}{U_3} = \ldots
$$

### General Formulas:

- Recursive relation:  
  $$U_n = U_{n-1} \cdot r$$
- Explicit formula or **n-th term**:  
  $$U_n = U_1 \cdot r^{n-1}$$



## 4. Geometric Series

A **geometric series** is the sum of the terms in a geometric sequence:

$$S_n = U_1 + U_2 + \ldots + U_n$$

Some example sums:

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_1 &= U_1 \\
S_2 &= U_1 + U_2 \\
S_3 &= U_1 + U_2 + U_3 \\
&\vdots \\
S_n &= U_1 + U_2 + U_3 + \ldots + U_n =\sum_{k=1}^{n} U_k
\end{aligned}
$$

### Sum Formula:

$$
S_n = \frac{U_1 (1 - r^n)}{1 - r}, \quad \text{if } r \ne 1
$$

### Remark

To solve some of the problems regarding geometric sequences and series you might need to find the solution of a system of equations.

## 5. Infinite Sum of a Geometric Series

If the common ratio $r$ satisfies $-1 < r < 1$, the infinite geometric series converges to a finite value (this means that the sum of an infinite number of terms is a real number):

$$S_\infty = U_1 + U_2 + U_3 + \ldots = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} U_k$$

### Formula:

$$S_\infty = \frac{U_1}{1 - r}$$

## 6. Quadratic Sequences

A **quadratic sequence** follows a rule involving a polynomial of degree 2. It can be identified by observing that **second differences** are constant. For example consider the following sequence:

$$5, 8, 13, 20,\ldots $$

<p style="text-align: center;">Second differences</p>
</div>



### General Form or n-th Term:

The n-th term for this type of sequence has the following form:

$$U_n = a n^2 + b n + c.$$

Our job is to determine the coefficients $a,b,c$ to find the general formula for the n-th term. To do this, we'll create a table using the general form, substituting the known values from the sequence. Then, we can solve for the coefficients one by one.
### Example:

Suppose the sequence has:

$$U_1 = 5,\quad U_2 = 8,\quad U_3 = 13, U_4=20, \ldots$$

Substitute into the general form, $U_n = an^2 + bn +c$:

<div style="text-align: center;">
<img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/Sequences/3.png" width="100%" alt="Quadratic sequence">
<p style="text-align: center;">Table generated by the sequence and the n-th term</p>
</div>


After completing the table, we identify from the first column that we can obtain the values we are looking for by isolating the variables, starting with the last row. In this case, we find that

$$2a = 2 \quad \Rightarrow \quad a = 1,$$

then by substituting this value into the third row, we get

$$3a+b=3 \quad \Rightarrow \quad 3(1)+b = 3  \quad \Rightarrow  \quad b=0,$$

and finally, using the second row, we find the last value,

$$a+b+c = 5 \quad \Rightarrow \quad 1+0+c =5 \quad \Rightarrow \quad c=4.$$


That implies that the $n$-th term of this sequence is given by the quadratic expression:

$$U _{n}= 1 n ^{2}+ 0 n+ 4 = n ^{2} + 4 .$$

**Final formula:**

$$U_n = n^2 + 4$$


## 7. Cubic Sequences

A **cubic sequence** follows a polynomial of degree 3 and has **constant third differences**.  For example consider the following sequence:

$$0,16,50,108,196, \ldots$$

### General Form or n-th Term:

The n-th term for this type of sequence has the following form:

$$U_n = a n^3 + b n^2 + c n + d$$

Once again our job is to determine the coefficients $a,b,c$ to find the general formula for the n-th term.

### Example:

Let us continue working with the previous cubic sequence:

<div style="text-align: center;">
<img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/Sequences/4.png" width="100%" alt="Cubic sequence">
<p style="text-align: center;">Table generated by the sequence and the n-th term</p>
</div>

After completing the table, we identify from the first column that we can obtain the values we are looking for by isolating the variables, starting with the last row. In this case, we find that

$$6a= 6 \quad \Rightarrow \quad a = 1,$$

then by substituting this value into the fourth row, we get

$$12a+2b =18 \quad \Rightarrow  \quad 12(1)+2b =18$$

hence $\b=3$.

Then, using the third row, we find

$$7a+3b+c=16 \quad \Rightarrow \quad 7(1)+3(3)+c = 16$$

Obtaining that $c=0$. And finally, using the second row, we find the last value

$$a+b+c+d=0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad 1+3+0+d=0 $$
Therefore $d=-4$. That implies that the $n$-th term of this sequence is given by the cubic expression:

$$U _{n}= 1 n ^{3}+ 3 n ^{2}+ 0 n+  -4 = n ^{3}+3n ^{2}-4 .$$

**Final formula:**

$$U_n = n^3 + 3n^2 - 4$$
