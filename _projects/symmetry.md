## What is Symmetry?

A figure has **symmetry** if it can be transformed (reflected or rotated) and still look exactly the same.

> Symmetry represents **balance** in a shape.

---

## Line of Symmetry (2D)

A **line of symmetry** is a line that divides a shape into two identical halves.

> If we fold the shape along this line, both halves match exactly.

<p align="center">
   <img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/Geometry/line_symmetry.png" width="600">
</p>


#### Examples

* A square has 4 lines of symmetry  
* A rectangle has 2 lines of symmetry  
* An equilateral triangle has 3 lines of symmetry

</p>

## Rotational Symmetry (2D)

A shape has **rotational symmetry** if it looks the same after being rotated around its center. The **center of rotation** is the point around which the shape is rotated.

<p align="center">
   <img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/Geometry/rotational_symmetry.png" width="600">
</p>

#### Order of Rotation


The **order of rotation** is the number of times a shape **maps onto itself** in a full turn $360^\circ$.

#### Examples

* Square → order 4 every $90^\circ$
* Equilateral triangle → order 3 every $120^\circ$ 
* Rectangle → order 2 every $180^\circ$

   <img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/Geometry/rotational_symmetry_examples.png" width="900">
</p>

## Symmetry in 3D Shapes

In three dimensions, symmetry becomes more complex.

#### Plane of Symmetry (3D)

A **plane of symmetry** divides a 3D object into two identical halves. It is the 3D version of a line of symmetry.


#### Examples

* Cube → 9 planes of symmetry  
* Sphere → infinitely many planes of symmetry  
* Box → 3 planes of symmetry 

<p align="center">
   <img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/Geometry/planes_symmetry.png" width="600">
</p>

---

## Rotational Symmetry in 3D

3D objects can also have rotational symmetry around an axis. The object looks the same after rotation around a line (axis).

### Examples

* Cylinder → rotational symmetry around its central axis  
* Sphere → rotational symmetry in all directions  
* Cube → rotational symmetry around several axes  

<p align="center">
   <img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/Geometry/rotational_symmetry_3d.png" width="600">

## Key Differences (2D vs 3D)

| Concept              | 2D Shapes              | 3D Shapes              |
|---------------------|----------------------|----------------------|
| Reflection          | Line of symmetry     | Plane of symmetry    |
| Rotation            | Around a point       | Around an axis       |

---


## Common Mistakes

* Confusing line and rotational symmetry  
* Counting incorrect lines of symmetry  
* Misidentifying rotational order
