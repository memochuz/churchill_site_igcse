## Length (Distance)

Common units for measuring length include:

* kilometer (km)  
* hectometer (hm)  
* decameter (dam)  
* meter (m)  
* decimeter (dm)  
* centimeter (cm)  
* millimeter (mm)  

These units are based on powers of 10, making conversions straightforward.


#### Conversions

Units are arranged as follows:

$$
\text{km} \rightarrow \text{hm} \rightarrow \text{dam} \rightarrow \text{m} \rightarrow \text{dm} \rightarrow \text{cm} \rightarrow \text{mm}
$$

Each step represents a factor of 10.


#### How to Convert

* Moving to the **right** (smaller units) → move the decimal point to the **right**  
* Moving to the **left** (larger units) → move the decimal point to the **left**  


#### Example

Convert $3.5 \, \text{m}$ to $\text{cm}$:

$$
\text{m} \rightarrow \text{dm} \rightarrow \text{cm}
$$

Move 2 steps to the right:

$$
3.5 \, \text{m} = 350 \, \text{cm}
$$

## Area

Area units are **squared**:


$$
\text{km}^{2} \rightarrow \text{hm}^{2} \rightarrow \text{dam}^{2} \rightarrow \text{m}^{2} \rightarrow \text{dm}^{2} \rightarrow \text{cm}^{2} \rightarrow \text{mm}^{2}
$$


#### How to Convert

* Moving to the **right** (smaller units) → move the decimal point to the **right two places** each time  
* Moving to the **left** (larger units) → move the decimal point to the **left two places** each time  

#### Example

Convert $3.5 \, \text{m}^{2}$ to $\text{cm}^{2}$:

$$
\text{m}^{2} \rightarrow \text{dm}^{2} \rightarrow \text{cm}^{2}
$$

Move 2 steps to the right, which means moving the decimal point 4 places to the right:

$$
3.5 \, \text{m}^{2} = 35000 \, \text{cm}^{2}
$$

## Volume

Volume units are **cubed**:

$$
\text{km}^{3} \rightarrow \text{hm}^{3} \rightarrow \text{dam}^{3} \rightarrow \text{m}^{3} \rightarrow \text{dm}^{3} \rightarrow \text{cm}^{3} \rightarrow \text{mm}^{3}
$$

#### How to Convert

* Moving to the **right** (smaller units) → move the decimal point to the **right two places** each time  
* Moving to the **left** (larger units) → move the decimal point to the **left two places** each time  

#### Example

Convert $3.5 \, \text{m}^{3}$ to $\text{cm}^{3}$:

$$
\text{m}^{3} \rightarrow \text{dm}^{3} \rightarrow \text{cm}^{3}
$$

Move 3 steps to the right, which means moving the decimal point 6 places to the right:

$$
3.5 \, \text{m}^{3} = 3500000 \, \text{cm}^{3}
$$

## Liters and Cubic Units

Another way to measure volume, especially for liquids, is by using liter-based units.

Common units for liquid volume include:

* kiloliter (kL)  
* hectoliter (hL)  
* decaliter (daL)  
* liter (L)  
* deciliter (dL)  
* centiliter (cL)  
* milliliter (mL)  

Like length units, these are also based on powers of 10, so conversions follow the same pattern.

Both liters and cubic units are used to measure volume. The key relationship between them is:

$$
1 \, \text{L} = 1000 \, \text{cm}^3
$$

or equivalently:

$$
1 \, \text{mL} = 1 \, \text{cm}^3
$$

This means that **liquid volume can be expressed using cubic units**, which allows us to connect measurements in geometry with real-life quantities like liquids.

#### Example

Convert $2 \, \text{L}$ to cubic decimeters:

We use the relationship:

$$
1 \, \text{L} = 1000 \, \text{cm}^3
$$

So:

$$
2 \, \text{L} = 2000 \, \text{cm}^3 = 2 \, \text{dm}^3
$$

## Mass (Weight)

For measuring mass, we use units such as:

* kilogram (kg)
* hectogram (hg)
* dekagram (dag)
* gram (g)
* decigram (dg)
* centigram (cg)
* milligram (mg)

Once again, these units are based on powers of 10, making conversions straightforward.

## Using the Rule of Three for Conversions

We can convert units using proportions.

#### Example

Convert $5 \, \text{km}$ to meters. First, we set up the proportion based on the known conversion:

$$
1 \, \text{km} \rightarrow 1000 \, \text{m}
$$

and 

$$
5 \, \text{km} \rightarrow x
$$

Therefore, we can write the proportion:

$$
\frac{1 \, \text{km}}{1000 \, \text{m}} = \frac{5 \, \text{km}}{x}
$$

Finally,solving for $x$ gives us:

$$
x = \frac{5 \cdot 1000}{1} = 5000 \, \text{m}
$$

## Compound Units

Compound units combine two units (usually division):

**Examples:**

* speed → $\frac{km}{h}$  
* density → $\frac{g}{cm^3}$  
* pressure → $\frac{N}{m^2}$  


#### Converting Compound Units

We convert **each part separately**.


###### Example: Speed

Convert $72 \, \frac{km}{h}$ to $\frac{m}{s}$:

**Step 1**: Convert km → m

$$
1 \, \text{km} \rightarrow 1000 \, \text{m}
$$

**Step 2**: Convert hours → seconds

$$
1 \, \text{h} \rightarrow 3600 \, \text{s}
$$

**Step 3**: Using the previous conversions, we can now find the equivalent speed in $\frac{m}{s}$:

$$
\frac{72 \, \text{km}}{1 \, \text{h}} = \frac{72000 \, \text{m}}{3600 \, \text{s}} = 20 \frac{\text{m}}{ \text{s}}
$$

Therefore, the final conversion is:

$$
72 \, \frac{\text{km}}{\text{h}} =  20 \frac{\text{m}}{ \text{s}}
$$

## Common Mistakes

* Forgetting to square or cube conversion factors  
* Mixing up units (e.g., cm and $\text{cm}^{2}$)  
* Not converting both parts in compound units  
* Skipping steps in the rule of three
