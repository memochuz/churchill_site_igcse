## Composite 2D Figures

#### Strategy

1. Divide into simple shapes.
2. Find each area.
3. Add or subtract as needed.


#### Example: Rectangle with a Semicircle on Top

<p align="center">
   <img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/Volumes_Areas/compositearea.png" width="600">
</p>

Step 1: Area of rectangle  
Step 2: Area of semicircle  

$$
Area = \underbrace{\frac{\pi \times r^{2}}{2}}_{\text{semicircle}} + \underbrace{b \times h}_{\text{rectangle}} 
$$

Therefore,

$$
Area = \frac{\pi \times (2.5)^{2}}{2} + 5 \times 10
$$

## Composite 3D Solids

#### Strategy

1. Break the solid into known shapes.
2. Calculate each volume separately.
3. Add volumes.
4. **For surface area**: subtract hidden/internal faces.


#### Example: Cylinder with a Hemisphere on Top

<p align="center">
   <img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/images/Volumes_Areas/compositevolume.png" width="600">
</p>


Volume:

$$
Volume = \underbrace{\pi r^2 h} _{\text{cylinder}}+ \underbrace{\frac{\frac{4}\pi \times  r^3}{3}}{2}}_{\text{semihesphere}} 
$$

Therefore,

$$
= \pi\times (2.5)^2 \times 10 + \frac{\frac{4}{3}\pi \times r^{3}}{2}
$$

Surface Area:

Do NOT include the circular face where they join.

$$
SA = \underbrace{\pi \times d \times h}_{\text{curved cylinder face}} + \underbrace{\pi \times r^2}_{\text{base}} + \underbrace{\frac{4\pi \times r^2}{2}}_{\text{half semihesphere}}
$$

Hence,
git
$$
Area = \pi \times 5 \times 10 + \pi \times (2.5)^{2}+ \frac{4\pi \times (2.5)^{2}}{2}
$$
(One base + curved cylinder + curved hemisphere)

# Important Exam Ideas

- Always check units.
- Volume → cubic units.
- Surface area → square units.
- Do not include internal faces.
- Draw a quick sketch to visualise hidden surfaces.
- Clearly show each step in composite shapes.

---

# Final Key Ideas

- Area measures 2D space.
- Volume measures 3D space.
- Surface area measures outer covering.
- Composite shapes require breaking into simpler shapes.
- For surface area, subtract overlapping faces.
- For volume, simply add all parts.
