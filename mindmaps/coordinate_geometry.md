# Coordinate Geometry

## Forms of Linear Equations

-   Standard Form
    - Formula: $ax + by = c$
    - Variables on one side, constant on the other.

- Gradient-Intercept Form
    - Formula: $y = mx + d$
    - **$m$**: Gradient (slope)
    - **$d$**: y-intercept (where line crosses y-axis)
- Gradient-Point Form
    - Formula: $y - y_0 = m(x - x_0)$
    - Useful with slope and one point $(x_0, y_0)$.
- Two-Point Form
    - Formula: $y - y_1 = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}(x - x_1)$
    - Used when two points $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$ are known.

## Key Calculations

- Gradient $m$
    - Formula: $m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$
    - Concept: $\frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}$ 
(Vertical change / Horizontal change).

- Midpoint
    - Formula: $M = \left(\frac{x_1 + x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right)$
    - Concept: Average of $x$ and $y$ coordinates, point halfway between two others.
- Distance
    - Formula: $d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$
    - Based on: Pythagora's Theorem (length of the hypotenuse).

## Point Verification

- Does a Point Belong to a Line?

    - **Step 1:** Substitute the coordinates $(x, y)$ into the line equation.

    - **Step 2:**
        - If equality is **true**: Point lies on the line.
        - If equality is **false**: Point is not on the line.
