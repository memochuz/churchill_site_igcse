# Simplifying Radicals
## Square Roots (Index 2)
### The goal is to find the simplest form
### Step 1: Find Prime Factors
- Break down the number under the radical into its prime factors.
- Example: $48 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 3$
### Step 2: Group Pairs
- Since the index is 2, group factors into pairs.
- Example: $\sqrt{(2 \times 2) \times (2 \times 2) \times 3}$
### Step 3: Simplify and Take Out
- For each pair, take one factor outside the radical.
- Multiply the outside factors.
- The remaining factor stays inside.
- Example: $2 \times 2\sqrt{3} = 4\sqrt{3}$

## Cube, Fourth, and Other Roots
### The same principles apply
### Step 1: Find Prime Factors
- Find the prime factorization of the number under the radical.
### Step 2: Group According to Index
- For a cube root (index 3), group factors of three.
- For a fourth root (index 4), group factors of four.
- And so on...
### Step 3: Simplify and Take Out
- For each group, take one factor outside the radical.
- The remaining factors stay inside.
- Example (Cube Root of $108$): $\sqrt[3]{(3 \times 3 \times 3) \times (2 \times 2)} = 3\sqrt[3]{4}$
